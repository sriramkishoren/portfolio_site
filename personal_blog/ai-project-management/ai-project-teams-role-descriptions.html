Common Roles in AI Project Teams

How Five Specialized Roles Form the Nucleus of Effective AI Capability Delivery

Discover the foundational delivery structure for building AI teams, exploring specialized role responsibilities, critical team interactions, and the realities of modern AI labor economics.

Based on lectures by Dr. Ian McCullough, Johns Hopkins University

AI Requires Labor Specialization

The Flash Team Model

As artificial intelligence has grown in maturity at an accelerating rate, it demands labor specialization. This represents the common delivery team used to build the Accenture Federal AI practice — providing the foundational delivery structure for AI project teams.

"It is not realistic for you to think that I'm going to hire an 'AI guy' and I'm going to deliver AI capability that makes sense."

  • Attempting to use a generalist approach may result in wasteful labor.
  • Top talent doing AI and machine learning is eager to specialize in developing or applying algorithms, not managing all aspects of a project.
  • Teaming algorithm specialists with data-familiar staff creates a vastly more productive partnership. Let's examine each role and how they interact.

Data Scientist

Senior Analytical Leader

Typically the senior person on the team. They hold the widest knowledge of different analytical techniques that can be brought to bear to solve a problem. Their primary focus is on building algorithms, not managing data.

Specialization Domains:

Computer Vision NLP Generative AI Forecasting Graph Analytics

Scale Insight: Specialization depends on scale and volume. Small budgets require generalists; sufficient volume enables specialized efficiency.

Education: Usually requires a Master's degree in data science, AI, CS, or similar. Highly difficult to train outside a graduate program.

Data Engineer

The Critical Foundation

Equipped with strong SQL and cloud engineering skills, they manage data so data scientists can focus purely on algorithms. This forms the most critical division of labor across all staffing models.

Labor Economics:

  • • Most commoditized and routine role
  • • Often the largest cost center
  • • Least creative, leading to job fit challenges and inflated salaries
  • • Salary inflation expected to slow due to new automation

Automation Impact: Symbolic generative AI is automating tasks without human error, achieving greater speed and accuracy.

Reskilling: Organizations may already have people doing database management tasks who are easily reskilled.

Domain Expert

Business Value Guardian

Sits with the team to guarantee an understanding of business value. They ensure the team is solving problems that make sense and interpreting industry-specific, proprietary data correctly.

Key Questions They Answer:

  • "Is the team focused on business problems that make sense?"
  • "Can they explain the data?"
  • "What is going to be useful at the end of the day?"

Engagement: May not be full-time. Often works within the business full-time and serves the AI project on-call, depending on project magnitude.

Data Viz Expert

Packaging For Impact

Responsible for decision support via tools like Tableau, Power BI, or Qlik Sense. They are not building the model; they package findings so people can consume and understand them.

Easiest role to train: Under proper supervision, a smart college grad with good communication skills can be trained in just a few months. Easiest role to hire and scale.

Substitution Note: Sometimes substituted with a software engineer if the team is developing an application rather than a dashboard.

Junior Analyst

Scaling Engine & Career Launchpad

Exists to scale the team and lower the price point. They are not generalists—they possess technical foundations but rotate to support all roles.

Minimum Skills:

Strong programming, calculus, math skills, and basic statistics.

"Look at skills, not the degree."

Four Interaction Paths (Career Discovery)

Works with Domain Expert:
Helps define initial project requirements.
Works with Data Engineer:
Figures out how to operationalize data.
Supports Data Scientist:
Executes algorithms and checks model performance.
Supports Data Viz Expert:
Assists during deliverable packaging.

Social Sciences Pipeline: Organizations are often surprised to find social science graduates with calculus and Python skills making great problem solvers here. As they work across these roles, they begin to learn what area they like best, often pursuing graduate education later.

The Most Important Division of Labor

This structural split is common across all staffing models, completely decoupling model development from data preparation.

Division of Labor

Data Scientist

  • Focus: Algorithms, problem-solving, and developing use cases.
  • Skills: Deep knowledge of varied analytical techniques and feature engineering.
  • Value: Applying specialized AI techniques to organizational problems.

Data Engineer

  • Focus: Moving, managing, cleaning, and preparing data.
  • Skills: Strong SQL, cloud engineering, and database management.
  • Value: Enables the data scientist to focus strictly on algorithms.

Team them up — creating a more productive partnership.

Labor Economics & Trends

Data Engineering Commoditization

The largest cost center and most routine role. Salaries are currently inflated, but new automation with symbolic GenAI puts downward pressure on future salary inflation.

Trainability Spectrum

Data Viz Easiest (Months)
Junior Analyst Moderate (Skills)
Data Science Hard (Master's)

Specialization at Scale

Scale and volume considerations dictate team design. Small budget equals generalists; sufficient volume allows for specializations, resulting in greater efficiency.

COVID Modeling Case Study

This end-to-end example highlights how the flash team maintains strict separation of concerns to deliver actionable decision support.

"They're not building the model — they're packaging it so people can consume and understand."

1. Data Scientist

Builds a highly accurate 21-day COVID case count forecast model.

2. Data Viz Expert (Dashboard)

Transforms the model output into a web-deployable dashboard.

3. Data Viz Expert (Alerts)

Adds SMS messaging tied to zip codes to track trending case increases.

4. End Users

Receive localized alerts based on 3-week forecasts, enabling them to "plan early".

AI Maturity

AI maturity drives specialization. Organizations cannot treat every project with a generic data scientist. Top talent wants to specialize in distinct domains.

Scale / Volume Rule: Generalists ← Small Budgets
Specialists ← Sufficient Volume (Efficiency)

Modern Extensions

Emerging roles extending the flash team model.

  • MLOps Eng: Bridges modeling & deployment.
  • AI Product Mgr: Extends domain expert with product discipline.
  • Prompt Eng: New GenAI data science sub-specialty.
  • Responsible AI: Addresses ethical governance & bias.

Career Pathway

Branching from Junior Analyst to specialist paths.

  • Data Science: Requires grad education.
  • Data Engineering: Build on SQL/cloud.
  • Data Viz: Deepen dashboard/BI skills.
  • Domain/PM: Leverage industry knowledge.

Effective AI teams require five specialized roles working in concert — not a single "AI guy."

The data scientist and data engineer form the most critical partnership. Junior analysts scale the team while discovering their career path. Domain experts keep AI focused on business value. And data visualization experts package findings so decisions can be made.

Build teams that specialize, hire for skills over degrees, and invest in the most important division of labor.
