The COVID Catalyst

During the COVID pandemic, there was a significant rise in remote work. Part of this was the need for AI and IT to enable distributed teams. Initially, organizations observed a short-term increase in productivity. However, there was an adverse impact on education and training — the assumption that people would use newfound free time for learning does not bear out empirically.

The pandemic compressed years of remote work adoption into weeks, forcing organizations to implement distributed infrastructure without the cultural adaptation that normally accompanies such transitions. The initial productivity gains masked emerging problems in collaboration, mentorship, and innovation that would surface over the following years.

SHORT-TERM GAIN
Productivity ↑
LONG-TERM COST
Education & Training ↓

To understand why, we need to examine the fundamental tension between efficiency and innovation.

C

Efficiency vs. Innovation

SIGNATURE FRAMEWORK — They Optimize Different Things

EFFICIENCY

How do you achieve a well-defined task consistently, quickly, at a cost-efficient point?

Requires: FORMAL LEADERSHIP
  • Management and supervision
  • Well-documented standards
  • Standards enforced by leaders with authority and resources
  • Ensuring people do what they are supposed to do

INNOVATION

How do you come up with new ways? Think outside the box? Novel solutions to novel problems?

Requires: INFORMAL LEADERSHIP
  • Not formally tasked or assigned
  • Know where resources are across the organization
  • Social capital from relationships with many people
  • Step in and solve problems when needed
The Balance

Not opposites — they optimize different things.

Strong formal leader → restricts informal leader's influence. Weak or absent formal leader → informal leader steps in and solves problems.

Remote work strengthens formal leadership structures but weakens the informal networks that drive innovation.

D

Proximity — The Irreplaceable Social Force

Proximity is one of the social forces that can promote both efficiency and innovation. When you bump into somebody at the coffee pot, have unprogrammed chats, or attend happy hours and social events, you talk about things that are not task-specific. That is where you learn about things that may not be important today but might help you be an informal leader further down the road.

WHAT PROXIMITY PROVIDES
  • Coffee pot conversations — unprogrammed, task-free
  • Happy hours and social events — relationship building
  • Hallway encounters — chance contact with new information
  • Shared physical space — familiarity and intimacy
WHAT REMOTE WORK LOSES
  • Lack of proximity → degraded informal leadership
  • Lack of intimacy → weakened organizational relationships
  • Non-task conversations disappear → social capital erodes
  • Virtual substitutes struggle to reproduce spontaneity
Learn things not important today → Become an informal leader tomorrow

THE MINDSET

University Culture

Do your own homework assignment. Do not partner with other people. Figure it out yourself or you are not valuable.

THE COST

Senior person at higher price point coding = Inefficient

Junior person spending hours online to avoid looking stupid = Inefficient

THE GAP

Senior leaders have knowledge and experience. Junior workers have energy and technical currency. But they lack the relationships to connect in hybrid remote teams.

"Nobody has really solved this problem yet."

We need to right-size the way we actually create intimacy and familiarity.

F

Reduced Normative Influence

Organizations set goals and values. People committed to the company and doing a great job get praised and rewarded. This creates a normative effect where other people want to be like that too — a company culture where people are motivated to making the organization better. When people are remote, that influence diminishes.

IN-OFFICE CHAIN
Goals Set → Great Work Praised → Others Motivated → Strong Culture
REMOTE CHAIN
Praise Less Visible → Reduced Commitment → Quiet Quitting → Eroding Performance

The quiet quitting phenomenon reflects the absence of social reinforcement mechanisms that connect individual effort to organizational purpose and peer recognition — not laziness, but a loss of ambient cultural signals.

G

Client Relationships & Retention

Client retention depends on relationships. People, especially non-technical clients, are not necessarily connoisseurs of the best AI — but they know who their friends are. They are more likely to buy from people they have relationships with that they are confident will get the job done.

THE FINE LINE
Nepotism ←→ Trust

Without on-site, in-person relationships, that client team trust degrades and makes it more difficult to retain work. This is important for retaining jobs for people and reducing turbulence in the job market.

Effective client relationships are built on demonstrated competence reinforced by personal rapport — not personal rapport alone. Remote engagement requires more deliberate relationship-building effort.

H

Innovation Requires Proximity

Innovation requires four essential elements — and remote work undermines the most critical one.

Cognitive Diversity
Inclusion
A Problem
Time + Chance Contact ⚠️

The time for people to just be together and chat about what is going on in a problem — and the chance contact — is essential for innovation to occur. With remote work trends, we have seen a degradation in that type of innovation.

Remote work primarily attacks element four — the time and chance contact. Efficiency improvements can mask innovation decline until the organization faces a novel challenge its weakened informal networks cannot address.

The Human Baseline Ethics Framework

SIGNATURE FRAMEWORK — AI vs. Human Error

"Ignorance is the architect of stagnation."

Fear-driven policies are overly focused on risk and overlook the human baseline. For any task in business, there is a human way and an AI way — but many look only at potential AI problems, completely ignoring that human processes are flawed themselves.

THE AI-ONLY VIEW

Common but Incomplete

  • "Can the AI make mistakes?"
  • "Can the AI be unfair?"
  • "Are there disadvantaged populations?"
  • "Let's not use AI."
THE HUMAN BASELINE VIEW

Comparative — The Right Question

  • Humans ARE biased — documented for centuries
  • Humans DO disclose private data
  • Humans are NOT transparent
  • Humans CANNOT explain their own decisions

Ethics is not about AI problems in isolation — it is comparing AI problems against manual human error.
J

Privacy, Transparency & Regulation

All the language we discuss about AI bias and ethical considerations is actually derived from centuries of well-documented evidence that human processes are flawed. We need a human baseline approach instead of looking at AI in isolation.

Privacy
Not disclosing data unnecessarily
Transparency
Why is AI making this decision?
Explainability
Can we explain to non-PhDs?
Regulation
Frameworks and guidelines
THE REGULATORY ASYMMETRY WARNING

We absolutely know that humans are biased, disclose privacy issues, are not transparent, and many times cannot explain their own decisions. Regulatory frameworks must not be so overly restrictive on AI and underly restrictive on manual processes that we allow people to have life-threatening issues because we have failed to adopt AI when we should have.

Technical Talent in North America

This does not mean there is no role for technical talent in North America. What motivates a client to buy? Sure, it could be personal relationships — golf, dinner, sports — but that is risky and hit-or-miss. A more sure bet: the client wants to talk to a technical leader who understands the key steps in delivering an AI project.

WHAT CLIENTS WANT
  • Someone who knows what they are talking about
  • Understands key steps in AI project delivery
  • Knows the risks
  • Can articulate the resources needed
  • Provides a sound strategy
THE EMERGING MIX
  • Onshore: Client-facing technical + domain expertise
  • Offshore: Technical execution + delivery
  • Industry soft skills less in demand for offshore
  • Technical skills in demand everywhere
  • Both must work together to deliver solutions
Technical credibility is a more sure bet than personal rapport alone.

Key Takeaways & Closing Reflection

REMOTE WORK IMPACT

COVID → Productivity ↑ but Proximity ↓ → Informal leadership ↓ → Innovation ↓ → Culture ↓ → Client trust ↓ → Junior workforce isolated → Normative influence lost → Quiet quitting → Eroding performance

EFFICIENCY-INNOVATION BALANCE

Efficiency (formal leadership) and innovation (informal leadership) optimize different things. Remote work strengthens formal structures but weakens the informal networks that drive innovation.

HUMAN BASELINE ETHICS

Compare AI against human baseline, not in isolation. Organ donation: 3,000 additional transplants through AI. Do not let regulatory asymmetry restrict AI while leaving flawed human processes unchecked.

OFFSHORE LABOR ECONOMICS

21% client-facing / 75% offshore / 4% executive. $200/hr onshore → $166/hr blended. Technical credibility retains client-facing value in North America. Mix of onshore + offshore teams must collaborate.

Productivity ↑ Short-Term Education ↓ $200/hr → $166/hr 3,000 Lives Saved 75% Offshore 21% Client-Facing

Remote work promised freedom and flexibility. It delivered both — but at a cost we are still measuring. The short-term productivity gains masked a deeper erosion: the loss of proximity that builds informal leadership, the degradation of normative influence that sustains organizational culture, the isolation of a junior workforce that does not know how to ask for help, and the weakening of client relationships that depend on trust built face-to-face.

Meanwhile, the same infrastructure that enabled domestic remote work opened the door to global labor arbitrage — shifting 75% of project delivery offshore and compressing blended rates from $200 to $166 an hour. The efficiency gains are real. The innovation losses are real too.

And in the ethical domain, we learned that the biggest risk is not AI imperfection — it is the refusal to adopt AI because we forgot that human processes are flawed themselves. 3,000 people received organ transplants because someone compared AI against the human baseline instead of examining AI in isolation.

Organizations that thrive in the remote work era will be those that deliberately reconstruct what proximity provided: the unprogrammed conversations, the cross-generational mentoring, the normative cultural signals, and the chance contacts that drive innovation.

Efficiency can be managed remotely. Innovation cannot — not yet. The organizations that solve the proximity problem will win. The ones that pretend it does not exist will wonder why they stopped innovating.
