The Talent-Position Matching Challenge

When an AI tool tries to match requested skills to available people for staffing a new project, a fundamental problem emerges. Searching for a common skill like Tableau returns results spanning multiple organizational names, different job families (each with various career steps), different project roles, and multiple HR partners. With no common manager or consistent career experience, finding the right person and pricing deals accurately becomes nearly impossible. Let's examine why this happens and how to fix it.

Hierarchical Skill Clustering

The analytical approach: using unsupervised learning on real HR data to discover natural skill groupings.

17 Role Families
1,300+ HR Database Skills

The Methodology

A skill is connected to another skill if 20 or more people share both skills. This filters out the long tail of obscure/niche skills and reveals what groups exist naturally. This approach discovered three general clusters.

The Pricing Problem

When a single functional skill comes attached to different compensation levels.

"Huge range of potential compensation"

  • Pricing based on the higher title (Data Scientist) means you are way higher, less competitive, and won't win the deal.
  • Staffing the higher title at the skill rate means you won't be profitable.
  • Solution: Separate rationalized roles for the specific skill. Price at skill value, deliver at that price point.

Transforming Chaos into Clarity

Talent-position matching transforms organizational chaos into operational clarity. When a single skill search returns people from different org units with different titles, different pay, and different managers, you cannot price deals consistently, staff projects optimally, or maintain pay equity.

Hierarchical skill clustering reveals how skills actually group in your workforce — not how HR categorized them. Rationalize your role families: 11 to 5, fragmented to focused. Build delivery models that connect rationalized roles to client-facing team structures with realistic scope, pricing, and staffing.

Ensure pay equity is based on skill and merit, not on how well someone negotiated a niche title. When role rationalization and delivery models have a mapping that makes sense, you can recruit, retain, and deliver with consistency — and everybody can be happy at the other end.
