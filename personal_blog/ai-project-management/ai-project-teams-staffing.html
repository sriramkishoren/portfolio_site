Dr. Ian McCullough, Johns Hopkins University

AI Impacts on Labor

We are going to be talking about artificial intelligence impacts on labor. One of the earliest explorations into AI project roles was conducted by Sam Huddleston at the Center for Army Analysis in the early 2010s.

The Core Insight: He divided tasks into a development environment and a production environment. There is value in doing development and testing out what you can possibly do with the data, but a separate set of skills is necessary to put solutions into production.

In addition to identifying four distinct roles, he identified the limitations that managers need to watch for to prevent suboptimal capability. Let's examine the two environments.

The Four Specialized Roles

Development

Data Scientist

King of the Development Environment

Figuring out different data and organizational problems. He or she should have a wide set of different skills to draw from and bring to bear given the requirements. Builds up different use cases.

Critical Limitation:

Education & Skill (Needs time for conferences, latest trends, advanced degrees to stay cutting edge).

Requirements Input

Requirements / Domain Expert

Driving the Problem

Somebody that is out in the field with the main experience. Actually driving the requirements and feeding them into the data scientist. Ensures solutions address real organizational problems.

Key Function:

Acts as the critical bridge between the operational business world and technical development.

Production

Software Engineer

The Productionizer

Receives handoffs from development to actually develop applications that productionize validated use cases in intelligent ways and have good user interfaces.

Critical Hand-off Issue:

Product Owner / Software Compatibility (Library mismatch between dev and prod environments).

Spans Both

Data Engineer

Managing the Flow

Looks at different internal/external data sources. Decides what to make available to the data scientist for development, and what to make production-ready in a platform for the software engineer.

Critical Limitation:

Vendor Lock & Data Access (License agreements, ability to do R&D, overly restrictive security policies).

Four Critical Bottlenecks

Management must monitor these limitations to prevent suboptimal capability.
"Treat these roles differently."

01

Education & Skill

Affects: Data Scientist (Development)

  • Are they engaging in conferences?
  • Do they have time to read latest trends?
  • Do they have advanced degrees?

"Not as necessary for your software engineer... It's really for the data scientist."

02

Product Owner Compatibility

Affects: Dev → Prod Handoff

CASE STUDY: The ERGM Example

Exponential Random Graph Models are useful for recommender systems. But their libraries exist in statistical languages like R, not in production software like Java, Python, or C++. The cost of converting algorithms becomes highly restrictive.

Are we developing things that don't have an equivalent library on the production environment?

03

Vendor Lock & Data Access

Affects: Data Engineer (Pipelines)

  • Limitation in serving realistic data.
  • Complex license agreements for data.
  • Freedom to do R&D on non-production data.
  • Overly restrictive security policies.
04

Training & Change Mgmt

Affects: End User Adoption

Getting production apps actually used by the people working in the business. Even beautifully engineered AI fails if adoption is ignored.

Requires dedicated training and organizational change management.

The Value Realization Principle

✗

THE FALLACY

Thinking that you begin and end with the data. Collecting data "in case."

✓

THE TRUTH

You begin and end with the business problem or the business value you're trying to achieve.

Key Takeaways

  • Two Environments: Development (experimentation) and Production (deployment) require fundamentally different skills.
  • Four Roles: Data Scientist, Requirements Expert, Software Engineer, Data Engineer.
  • Four Bottlenecks: Education, Software Compatibility, Vendor Lock, and Change Management.
  • Value Realization: Do not start with data. Begin and end with the business value.

"AI project staffing requires understanding two distinct environments, four specialized roles, and four critical bottlenecks. Begin and end with business value — not data. Treat each role differently, address each bottleneck specifically, and build teams that bridge the development-to-production divide."
