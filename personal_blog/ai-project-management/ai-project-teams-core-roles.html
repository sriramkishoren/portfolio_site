There Is No Single "AI Guy"

AI project success requires bringing together diverse, specialized skill sets. Different competencies must come together intelligently to deliver AI success.

The Challenge: No industry standard exists for AI team roles. If you search for common AI titles, you will find a wide variety of terms. This infographic explores four distinct frameworks to help organize and understand these essential project teams.

ESTABLISHED

Data Engineer

The Foundation Builder

Creates the vital data foundation. They move data around, ensure data correctness, and model databases for easy retrieval.

Key Realities:

  • Up to 80% of any IT project involves data engineers managing queries and data structure.
  • 80% of a data scientist's time is typically spent doing data cleaning—tasks a data engineer could do more readily.

Responsibilities: Data integration, modeling, optimization, quality, self-service. The largest role in industry regardless of AI use.

CORE ROLE

Data Scientist

The Algorithm Expert

Focuses on algorithms, deep learning, and math. They look at the use case to determine the right datasets, algorithms, and features.

Core Capabilities:

  • Can build, test, and tune AI models.
  • Makes end-to-end small use cases work successfully.
  • The role most commonly tied to AI in labor trends.

Responsibilities: Identifies use cases, determines appropriate datasets and algorithms, experiments, and builds AI models.

EMERGING

AI Architect

The Glue Role

Acts as the bridge between data scientists, data engineers, developers, operations, and business unit leaders to govern and scale AI.

Skill Convergence:

Enterprise Architecture Cloud Engineering Operations Research AI Skills

Industry Context: Relatively new role. Very few at-scale AI projects are deployed yet, but demand is rapidly increasing as models move to cloud.

EMERGING

ML Engineer

The Deployment Specialist

Specializes in optimizing AI deployment across the enterprise, taking validated models and ensuring production readiness at scale.

Key Focus Areas:

  • Effective scaling of AI models.
  • Ensuring a continuous feedback loop.
  • Optimizing enterprise integration.

Industry Context: Paired with the AI Architect as a relatively new discipline. Growth will surge as organizations transition beyond the proof-of-concept phase.

Industry Maturity Context

Most of the industry remains focused on proof-of-concept testing with light-touch data science. Very few at-scale AI projects are fully deployed.

As organizations mature, demand shifts from data scientists who build pilots, toward ML Engineers and AI Architects who productionize and scale systems.

Cross-Functional Comm.

Capability Pathway

Start with data foundations, add science, then scale to architecture and deployment.

1. ASSESS

Map current team capabilities and gauge organization maturity.

2. PRIORITIZE

Build Data Engineering foundations, then add Data Science for PoCs.

3. CROSS-FUNCTION

Ensure specialized PM knowledge and solid domain expertise exist.

4. SCALE

Move from multi-hat roles to specialized Architects and ML Engineers.

The Core Principle

"AI success requires bringing together diverse, specialized skill sets — there is no single AI role that does everything. Understanding these roles is the first step to building effective AI teams."
