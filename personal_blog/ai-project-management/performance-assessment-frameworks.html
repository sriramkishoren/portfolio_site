The Performance Assessment Landscape

Opening Framing

There are a number of performance assessment frameworks in the AI industry, each with different strengths, weaknesses, and preferences. They are all important to consider in how they actually impact performance, specifically for an AI team.

KPIs OKRs 360-Degree Balanced Scorecards SMART Goals Agile Reward Indicator

The choice of framework signals what the organization values, how contributions will be measured, and whether the system acknowledges the unique nature of AI work. No single framework works for every AI organization — the optimal choice depends on whether the organization prioritizes efficiency or innovation.

The Seven Frameworks Landscape

Signature Visual 1

Top-Down / Rigid Bottom-Up / Flexible

KPIs

TOP-DOWN · MEASUREMENT

Business performance metrics. Leader defines benchmarks. Rigid for employees but consistent over time. Best for efficient organizations.

OKRs

BOTTOM-UP · GOAL-SETTING

Employees define objectives. Self-selected, flexible, tailored to roles. Collective alignment. Best for innovative organizations.

360-Degree Feedback

MULTI-DIRECTIONAL · FEEDBACK

Asking subordinates, peers, and seniors to provide feedback on you. Multi-directional input for holistic view.

Balanced Scorecards

MULTI-DIMENSIONAL · MEASUREMENT

Decompose success into common dimensions: financials, customer feedback, growth of the account. Holistic performance view.

SMART Goals

STRUCTURED · GOAL-SETTING

Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound. Making goals very clear, fair, and transparent to people.

Agile Methodologies

ADAPTIVE · ITERATIVE

Assessing performance with flexibility. Iterative, adaptive approach suited to evolving project scopes and rapid delivery cycles.

Reward Indicator (Force Distribution)

Dr. McCullough’s Preferred

CALIBRATION · CROSS-MANAGER · OVERLAYS ANY FRAMEWORK

Force-distributed points system. More subjective but unlocks aggregated judgment. Multiple managers and people have a conversation about ratings to achieve a very diverse and fair assessment. Can be layered on top of either KPI or OKR systems.

D

The Reward Indicator System

Force-Distributed Judgment

50 People × 5 Points = 250 Total
EQUAL

Everyone gets 5 — no differentiation

EXTREME

One person gets 250, everyone else zero

REALITY

Give a 6? Someone gets a 4. Give a 7? More get 4s.

You do not want to give somebody a three because you feel bad — so you spread it out. Multiple managers and multiple people all have a conversation or discussion about the ratings, unlocking that aggregated judgment.

Result: A very diverse and fair assessment of what the ratings are — calibrated by collective dialogue rather than individual manager opinion. This addresses grade inflation and forces articulation of why someone deserves more or fewer points.

KPIs: The Top-Down Assessment System

Signature Visual 2

TOP-DOWN: Leader Defines What Matters

Strengths

  • Business performance metrics compared against benchmarks
  • Really good at measuring progress over time — same measure every quarter or year
  • Ensures clearly articulated vision, goals, and priorities
  • Ensures efficiency and doing things the way they are supposed to

The Tension

  • Somewhat rigid and inflexible for employees
  • Technical staff tends to feel they are unfairly benchmarked against project leaders
  • Technical staff feels their contributions are not as valued
  • Project leaders tend to think it is more fair
  • Project leaders are open to concessions for technical benchmarks
  • Technical staff will still feel unfairly benchmarked

BEST FIT: Efficient Organizations

Formality · Clear defined task and purpose · Standards and procedures

F

OKRs: The Bottom-Up Assessment System

Signature Visual 3

BOTTOM-UP: Employees Define What Matters

THE ALIGNMENT PROCESS

Objective: Deliver this AI system that does X, Y, and Z

Employee A: “I can take task X and achieve A, B, and C”

Employee B: “I can do Y”

Employee C: “I can do X too, and help by doing D, E, and F”

Collectively deciding how to achieve success together as a team

Strengths

  • Open and flexible standards tailored to roles
  • Everybody self-selects what they are going to do
  • Share OKRs with each other to get more creative and interested
  • No expectation to do what someone in another role does — own unique roles
  • Everyone comes to agreement on objectives and alignment

The Tension

  • Staff feels the assessment is not fair or consistent because different people have different tasks
  • The mirror image of the KPI problem: KPIs feel unfairly rigid; OKRs feel unfairly flexible

BEST FIT: Innovative Organizations

Diverse roles and functions · Unique custom problem solving · Creative collaboration

Assessment Tied to Recruiting & Retention

The Critical Connection

Performance assessment is highly tied to recruiting and retention. It is not just the performance appraisal system that we need to pay attention to — we also need to look at what other things we can do as leaders to recruit and retain high quality people to deliver the best product for our clients.

The performance assessment system communicates career commitment and the career development process. It creates a path where people can see that there is success at the company for them if they stay. The system is the primary communication mechanism about what success looks like and whether the employee’s future is valued.

The Six Key Retention Drivers

Signature Visual 5

Career Development & Growth

94%

94% of employees say they will stay longer at a business if they are career vested. The assessment system communicates career commitment and creates a path where people see success if they stay.

Challenging Projects

59%

Engaged employees committed to client delivery are 59% less likely to seek new opportunities. Project engagement is a powerful retention force.

Recognition & Reward

44%

44% cite lack of recognition as a key reason to leave. Ambiguous criteria means you hear about the right-hand man, not who makes the best impact. Best employees become invisible — filtered by middle management.

Advanced Tech Stack & Tools

Some employees are really motivated by access to advanced tech stack and tools. Creating opportunities for innovation is not just IRAD investment — it is also retention of high value employees. If you make them excited about innovation, and that growth converts to differentiated value-added sales for clients, that is a win-win.

Positive Work Environment

56%

56% cite positive culture as a reason they stay. A positive culture includes teamwork, fairness, opportunity to grow, being challenged, and liking the people they work with.

Flexible Work Arrangements

89%

89% of HR professionals agree flexible work improves retention. Work-life balance is culturally defined — it is also about hiring the right people that are a right fit for your clients.

J

The Recognition & Visibility Problem

The Invisible Employee

Ambiguous Criteria → Middle Management Filters → Best Impact Employees Invisible

What you hear about are the employees that are the right-hand man of somebody working for you — not necessarily who is making the best impact in the organization. The best contributors are invisible because they are always filtered by middle management.

You need solid ways to be objective and clear, to actually recognize and reward all employees. Employees know who does better or worse on a project — they have an idea about it. Recognize and reward in a transparent, fair way.

Recognize your own limitations and your ability to know who they are in your organization.

K

Compensation: The Last Resort

Problems with Compensation

“Compensation is probably the last resort in recruiting and retention.”

Most organizations default to compensation as the first tool for retention. The lecture argues this is exactly backwards. Here are five reasons why compensation-first strategies fail.

L

Diminishing Returns: French & Raven

Bases of Power Research

French and Raven — brilliant researchers on organizational incentives — found five (now six) bases of power, or ways you can leverage influence over people working for you.

They found that punishment and reward are both characterized by diminishing returns. You have to keep increasing the reward or punishment to achieve the same level of behavior.

It is not scalable. If compensation is your primary tool for organizational commitment, it cannot scale — or you will have exploding labor costs.

Reward must increase while behavioral response flattens

M

Intrinsic Motivation: The Federal AI Vision

Purpose Over Paycheck

Compensation is a short-term solution that does not address any underlying issues with job satisfaction. It ignores intrinsic motivation — are they doing the work because they care about it, care about the client, care about the difference it makes in people’s lives, or are they doing it because of the money?

“Americans deserve an AI-powered government. We should not have to wait for benefits. Our military and law enforcement should not go in harm’s way without the best insights. We should not tolerate bias and discrimination for manual processes. Americans deserve AI.”

The people who worked for me were focused on giving American government the best AI they could because it is making their country and their society better. Their main motivation is not the financial paycheck at the end of the day.

N

Inequity, Unsustainability & Market Vulnerability

Three Pillars of Compensation Failure

Inequity & Resentment

  • Very difficult to pay everybody the same
  • Different experience, skill sets, distractors, efforts
  • Not a perfect science
  • Inevitably leads to inequity and resentment
  • If compensation is the main measure of value, hard feelings affect retention

Financial Unsustainability

  • Leads to budget strain and a culture of escalating salary
  • Attractive costs of offshore and nearshore markets result in fewer local jobs
  • Always a place people can go for lower cost labor
  • As more people enter the workforce, this continues to change
  • Financial stability of compensation-first incentives is flawed

Market Vulnerability

  • Market fluctuations in trends impact pay
  • You do not want a bloated compensation bubble
  • Cannot win additional work at that price point and salaries have to come down
  • A real concern and real fear
  • In 2023, many companies saw this as the labor market adjusted in this space

Key Takeaways & Closing Reflection

Synthesis

Seven Frameworks

KPIs, OKRs, 360-Degree Feedback, Balanced Scorecards, SMART Goals, Agile Methodologies, and the Reward Indicator — all with different strengths, weaknesses, and preferences for AI team performance.

KPI vs. OKR

Efficient organizations → KPIs (top-down, consistent, technical staff tension). Innovative organizations → OKRs (bottom-up, flexible, consistency perception). The choice determines what is valued.

Reward Indicator

Force-distributed points across 250-point pools. Multiple managers discuss ratings. Unlocks aggregated judgment for diverse, fair assessment.

94% Career Vested 59% Engagement 44% Recognition 56% Culture 89% Flexibility 2023 Bubble

Performance assessment frameworks are not interchangeable tools — they are strategic choices that communicate what the organization values, shape how AI talent perceives its contributions, and directly drive retention or attrition. Key Performance Indicators serve efficient organizations by providing consistent benchmarks, but technical staff will feel unfairly measured against project leaders. Objectives and Key Results serve innovative organizations by empowering employee agency, but the flexibility creates perceptions of inconsistency. The Reward Indicator system, with its force-distributed points and cross-manager dialogue, offers a powerful calibration mechanism that unlocks aggregated judgment and achieves diverse, fair assessment.

But frameworks alone are not enough. Ninety-four percent of employees stay longer when career vested — the assessment system must communicate that investment. Forty-four percent leave over lack of recognition — ambiguous criteria makes the best people invisible, filtered by middle management. And compensation, the tool most organizations reach for first, is actually the last resort. French and Raven’s research demonstrates that reward suffers diminishing marginal returns — it is not scalable, it ignores intrinsic motivation, it leads to inequity, and it creates compensation bubbles that the 2023 labor market painfully deflated.

The organizations that choose frameworks aligned to their organizational character, that build career paths people can see, that surface the contributions middle management filters out, that invest in innovation and culture alongside fair assessment, and that lead with purpose rather than compensation — those organizations will retain the AI talent that makes the difference. The ones that treat assessment as a form to fill out and compensation as the answer to every retention question will watch their best people walk out the door.
