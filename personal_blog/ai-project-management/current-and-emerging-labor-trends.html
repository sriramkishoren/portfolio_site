Section B

Four Key AI Use Cases in Business

The foundational categories that capture virtually every commercial AI application

⚙️

Automation

Use Case 1
Reduces backlog — e.g., patent claims processing: even 17–20% time savings can clear backlogs without workforce reduction
Shortens customer wait times through efficient software applications
Frees employees for higher-value, more thoughtful work
Targets repetitive, error-prone, or monotonous tasks
Claims Processing Data Entry Manufacturing Doc Review
🎯

Judgment

Use Case 2 · Decision Support
Narrows the scope of search for insights and business growth opportunities
Identifies high-probability sales leads and receptive clients
Consumer applications: product recommendations, entertainment curation, price comparison
Helps organizations make faster, data-driven decisions
Lead Scoring Risk Assessment Demand Forecasting
💬

Interactive

Use Case 3 · Experience Delivery
Personalization: knowing what customers want before they do
Real-time curation on shopping platforms
More human-feeling automated customer service systems
Reduces wait times — faster responses without holding
"Liquid Expectations": when one service sets a high bar, customers carry that expectation universally
Chatbots Rec Engines Voice Assistants Adaptive UI
🛡️

Responsibility

Use Case 4 · Risk & Ethics
Underlies all other three use cases as a foundational layer
Addresses: copyright violations, product liability, assembly line failures, human safety
Emerging market for AI responsibility audits in organizations
Includes risk mitigation, benefit maximization, and ethical governance
AI Auditing Bias Monitoring Safety Cert
💡 "Just about every type of AI application can be categorized into one of these four use cases." — Dr. McCullough

Section D

Design Thinking Approach to AI Implementation

A structured, people-centered methodology for prioritizing AI investments

1

Document Current Processes

Map existing workflows, regulations, and restrictions

2

Crowdsource Complexity

Gather 12+ stakeholders; use Post-It exercises to identify process steps

3

Rate Each Step

Assess data complexity and workload complexity using aggregated judgment

4

Plot on Matrix

Visualize where each step falls on the automate vs. augment spectrum

5

Prioritize Implementation

Quick wins first (automate low-complexity), then strategic augmentation

6

Vendor-Agnostic Recs

Identify solutions independent of specific technology providers

🫙

Aggregated Judgment & the Jelly Bean Effect

Individual guesses about complexity may be wildly inaccurate
But averaging ~12+ people's estimates produces surprisingly accurate results
Grounded in the Central Limit Theorem — averages become normally distributed and more accurate with more data
This crowdsourced assessment is a powerful, low-cost method for complexity evaluation

Section F

Generative AI Disruption

Blurring the boundaries between human-only and machine capabilities

⚡

The Boundary Shift

Generative AI has surprised the field by moving into what was considered human-only territory
Creativity, judgment, summarization, and content generation were previously seen as uniquely human capabilities
This encroachment creates both opportunity and anxiety in the labor market
Workers feel threatened because the "safe zone" of human-only skills is shrinking
Long-term effects on labor markets remain unknown and are actively being studied
Organizations must proactively rethink which tasks truly require human involvement
Section G

Automation ≠ Job Elimination

The Workforce Elevation Model — Automation as a path to better, not fewer, jobs

🔧

Tasks Best Suited for Automation

High room for human error
High monotony / low retention roles
Dangerous or hazardous work environments
Tasks people either aren't good at or don't want to do
🚀

Workforce Elevation Outcomes

Displaced workers redeployed to higher-value tasks
Elevated quality and effectiveness of overall workforce
Improved employee satisfaction and retention
Organizations become more competitive and innovative

💡 "Automating a manual workflow does NOT mean laying people off — it means elevating your workforce."

Section H

Liquid Expectations

The new consumer standard — When one industry raises the bar, all industries must follow

🌊

The Ripple Effect

When one industry delivers exceptional speed and convenience (e.g., ordering a product and receiving it by morning), consumers carry that expectation to every other interaction — creating a rising tide that lifts the bar across all sectors.

🏥 Healthcare 🏛️ Government / DMV 📋 Insurance 🎢 Hospitality / Theme Parks 💳 Financial Services 🎓 Education
AI becomes essential to meet these rising cross-industry expectations
Organizations that fail to keep pace risk losing customer trust and loyalty
This creates a competitive pressure loop that accelerates AI adoption across all sectors
Section I · Enhanced

Key Labor Market Shifts Driven by AI

Complementary insights on how AI is transforming the workforce landscape

1

Skills-Based Hiring

Employers increasingly value AI literacy and demonstrated skills over traditional degree requirements and credentials.

2

Rise of the "Hybrid Worker"

Professionals combining domain expertise with AI tool proficiency become the most valuable employees in any organization.

3

New Job Categories Emerging

AI trainers, prompt engineers, AI ethicists, model auditors, human-in-the-loop specialists, AI implementation consultants.

4

Gig Economy & AI

AI-powered platforms match freelancers to tasks with greater precision; on-demand labor markets continue to grow.

5

Upskilling Imperative

Organizations must invest in continuous learning programs to keep their workforce relevant and competitive.

6

Geographic Redistribution

Remote AI-enabled work redistributes opportunities beyond traditional tech hubs to new regions and communities.

7

Wage Polarization Risk

High-skill AI-complementary roles may see wage growth while routine roles face downward pressure — widening the economic divide.
