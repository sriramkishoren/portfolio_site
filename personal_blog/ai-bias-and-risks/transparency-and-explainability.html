This lecture focuses on the critical distinction between Transparency and Explainability. Let's explore why these are not the same thing.

The Core Distinction

These are independent properties. A model can be transparent without being explainable, and explainable without being transparent.

Transparency

HOW it works

Exposing the models, algorithms, and data so observers can see exactly what components make up the system.

Explainability

WHY it decided

Providing clear, human-understandable reasons for individual decisions and system actions.

Four Real-World Scenarios

Scenario 1: Financial Institution

A decision tree model used to approve loans.

Example Output

"Loan DENIED: Credit score < 700 AND debt-to-income ratio> 40%"

✓ Explainable Provides clear reasons for each decision based on the tree structure.
✗ Not Transparent Proprietary data and hidden criteria prevent full understanding of data sources.

Bias risk: There is no way to have a uniformly fair system; bias can hide behind explainability.

Scenario 2: Image Recognition

A deep neural network classifying images.

The Setup

Fully open source on GitHub: all model weights, architectures, training processes, and hyperparameters are publicly documented.

✓ Transparent Every aspect of the model's development is openly available and accessible.
✗ Not Explainable Understanding why it classified an image as a cat is not interpretable due to network complexity.

The takeaway: You can see everything, but still not understand why it decided what it did.

Scenario 3: Stock Price Prediction

A machine learning pipeline for financial forecasting.

The Reality

Pipeline design is fully disclosed. However, it uses complex ensemble methods with differently weighted models.

✓ Transparent Preprocessing, feature engineering, and model selection steps are entirely documented.
✗ Not Explainable It is hard to attribute specific factors to individual predictions (e.g., why a stock is predicted to rise).

Complexity barrier: Ensemble methods mix variables so intricately that distinct explanations are lost.

Scenario 4: Healthcare System

A rule-based expert system for medical recommendations.

Example Output

"Take Medication X because blood pressure is above [level] and history of heart disease."

✓ Explainable Explanation is clear, understandable, and based on predefined specific rules.
✗ Not Transparent The development process, criteria for rule creation, and what rules were omitted are hidden.

Hidden flaws: Bias can exist even when fully explainable if the rules themselves stem from biased demographic data.

The Critical Question

"How important is explainability if it means degraded performance? How responsible are high-performing algorithms if we can't explain their conclusions?"

The Goal of XAI

The idea of Explainable AI is not to force a binary choice between accuracy and interpretability. It's to push the trade-off curve to the upper right to achieve better interpretability AND better accuracy.

The Human Baseline

Human Decision-Making

  • Transparent? RARELY
  • Unbiased? NO (High Bias)
  • Explainable? RARELY
  • Consistent? NO

"Most people can't give you a good understanding of why they make the decisions they do."

AI Decision-Making

  • Transparent? VARIES
  • Unbiased? VARIES
  • Explainable? VARIES
  • Consistent? YES

Performance depends entirely on model type, transparency, and design choices.

"Oftentimes humans aren't necessarily better than what the AI system would provide."

The NIST Framework

Important standard criteria for responsible AI explanation, especially for government work.

Responsible AI Explanation

Explanation

System delivers accompanying evidence or reasons for ALL outputs, providing justification.

Meaningful

Provides explanations that are understandable to individual users. Avoids overly technical jargon.

Accuracy

Correctly reflects the system's actual process generating the output. Zero fabrication.

Knowledge Limits

System only operates under conditions for which it was designed when outputting confidence.

Key Takeaways

The TRUSTED Framework is Comprehensive Encompasses 7 principles: Transparent, Respect for Privacy, Unbiased, Sustainable, Traceable, Explainable, Dependable.
Transparency ≠ Explainability The 4 scenarios prove a model can expose its code without explaining its reasoning, or explain its output without sharing its data.
Push the Curve with XAI Don't accept the accuracy-interpretability trade-off as final. Use XAI techniques to push toward better performance AND understanding.
Meet the NIST Standards Ensure explanations are backed by evidence, meaningful to users, completely accurate, and respect knowledge limits.

"Transparency shows HOW a model works.
Explainability shows WHY it decided.

Responsible AI requires both — and the wisdom to know which gaps to address first."
