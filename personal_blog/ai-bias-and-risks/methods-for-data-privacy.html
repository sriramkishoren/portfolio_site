Data Encryption

Encrypting data at rest and in transit is essential to protect it from unauthorized access. You must ensure you are using strong encryption standards.

Data at Rest
Data in Transit
Extends with: AES-256 (Rest), TLS 1.3 (Transit), Key Management

Role-Based Access

A policy to restrict data based on the roles and responsibilities of users. You are not just letting everybody get access to data just because.

Authorized Role Access Granted
Unrelated Role Access Denied
Extends with: Principle of Least Privilege, Access Reviews

Data Masking & Anonymization

You may use data masking to obscure specific data in a database to protect it from unauthorized use, or to anonymize it and protect it from being identifiable.

The Zip Code Context Challenge

Large Population Zip Code

Looking at aggregated risk of disease is generally safe.

Individual data points blend in

Small Population Zip Code

Very few people live here. Individuals become visible.

Risk of privacy violation

Solution: Aggregate Contextually

The policy must require aggregation with neighboring zip codes. You still get the same informational value, but you successfully protect personally identifiable information.

Data Minimization

Limit the collection of personal data to what is strictly necessary for the intended purpose. Organizations often collect everything they can for "maybe someday."

The Deliberate Approach

Be forced into an exercise to define the intended goals of your AI system first. Then determine exactly what data is needed to drive those goals.

Better Privacy
Lower Costs
20% : Data You Actually Need
80% : Data Never Used

Most organizations don't know which 20% they actually need.

Retention Policies

Implemented to make sure we do not keep data any longer than we have to.

Collect safely
Use for intended purpose
Delete when finished

Privacy by Design

Incorporate privacy into the way we design and develop products from the beginning, so we don't have to collect personal identifiable information if we don't have to.

Retrofitting privacy later
Built into the foundation
The Breakthrough

Mathematical Formal Methods

Probably one of the most exciting breakthroughs you won't hear a lot about. Using mathematical formal methods, like category theory, creates inherently privacy-protecting queries.

Case Study: Global Ride-Sharing (Uber)

1

The Problem

Different privacy requirements globally. Each metro area developed data structures independently.

2

The Data Complexity

Drivers (licenses, insurance) vs. Passengers (credit cards). Massive amounts of required PII.

The Solution

Mathematical formal methods sit between the query and the data structures.

The Result

Privacy protection is built INTO the query. Violations are mathematically impossible.

"Oftentimes much more secure than competing methods like blockchain."

Incident Response Planning

Develop and maintain an incident response plan to address any data breaches promptly. Under regulations like GDPR, you have a limited time window from when you are aware of a breach until you must notify users.

Detect
Contain
Eradicate
Promptly Notify

GDPR mandates notification within tight timeframes (e.g., 72 hours).

You need a plan in place to act quickly.

What is your data, anyway?

With the advent of Generative AI, this has become a murky question.

Deepfake Likeness

A well-known actress had her likeness used in pornographic videos created by others making a model.

"Is a synthetic likeness necessarily HER data? Do model creators have rights to their output?"

Broadcast Exploitation

Another actress had explicit deepfakes broadcast publicly over the internet.

"A highly invasive use of data—but legally undefined whether a synthetic likeness qualifies as personal data."

Key Case

The OpenAI Voice Clone

Scarlett Johansson explicitly refused to license her voice. OpenAI developed a synthetic voice so similar that close friends couldn't tell the difference.

Unresolved Questions:

  • Prove copying vs. independent creation?
  • Right to prevent use?
  • Right to seek damages?
  • Can you own your voice in the AI age?

Complementary Extensions

Additional methods that seamlessly extend and complement the core toolkit discussed above.

Tokenization

Replaces sensitive data with non-sensitive tokens maintaining format.

Extends: Encryption

Data Loss Prevention

Monitors and controls movement to prevent leaks.

Extends: Access Control

Zero Trust

Requires verification for every single access request.

Extends: RBAC

Audit Logging

Records who accessed what data, when, and why.

Extends: Incident Response

Consent Management

Technical platforms managing user consent preferences.

Extends: Privacy by Design

Differential Privacy

Adds mathematical noise to prevent individual identification.

Extends: Masking
