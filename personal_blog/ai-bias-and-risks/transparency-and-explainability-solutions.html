Transparency and Explainability Solutions

Practical Techniques for Making Complex AI Models Understandable — LIME and SHAP in Action

Based on lectures by Dr. Ian McCullough, Johns Hopkins University

How do we make the unexplainable explainable?

Deep learning models, particularly convolutional neural networks (CNNs), are notoriously difficult to interpret. As models grow larger and more accurate, their internal decision-making becomes opaque.

Two key techniques — LIME and SHAP — provide practical solutions. Let's explore them through real-world healthcare applications, starting with a CNN designed to detect early-onset glaucoma.

LIME: Local Interpretable Model-agnostic Explanations

How LIME Works

LIME creates a locally interpretable approximation of a complex model's behavior around a specific prediction.

  • 1. Manipulates different feature weights
  • 2. Observes how changes affect the output data
  • 3. Identifies which factors contribute more or less
  • 4. Displays the relative weights of those factors

The "Plug-in" Advantage

LIME operates as an additional layer added to an existing framework. It acts as a plug-in that interfaces with the model's inputs and outputs. The complex underlying model does not need to be modified to gain explainability.

The Glaucoma Detection Pipeline (Stages A–E)

Adding an explainability layer to a notoriously difficult deep learning model.

A

Data Preprocessing

Examining earlier images of the eye (BMO-overview and BMO-based disc photography) from patients later diagnosed with glaucoma. Images are cropped and standardized.

B

Dataset Partitioning

Total n=501 split into training and test sets. Categories include healthy patients (GS, n=255) and patients who develop early normal-tension glaucoma (early NTG, n=246).

C

CNN Architecture

Images run through a convolutional neural network (27,122 parameters) containing multiple layers:

Input (224x224x3) → Conv2D → MaxPooling → Conv2D → MaxPooling → Conv2D → MaxPooling → Fully-Connected Dense
E

Explainable AI Layer (LIME)

Inserted between the model and diagnosis, LIME manipulates feature weights to reveal what drives the CNN's decision.

Feature Weights Identifies which factors contribute more or less, displaying their relative weights.
Image Regions Returns exactly which areas of the eye image drive the early-onset decision.
Clinical Validation Allows clinicians to verify if identified features have scientific rationale.

"Opens up new pathways for discovery — could lead to new insights into diagnosis and treatment."

D

Diagnosis Output

The final classification: GS (healthy), GF, or early NTG. Thanks to Stage E, this output is now explainable.

SHAP: SHapley Additive exPlanations

While LIME excels at highlighting local image regions, another powerful approach is the SHAP graph. Based on game theory, SHAP visualizes feature importance by showing how discriminatory each variable is in driving the model's outcome.

Bias Detection & Context Evaluation

When features like Age and Gender appear discriminatory in a SHAP plot, practitioners must evaluate for ethical concerns.

"Usually in healthcare outcomes, age is not necessarily an age-assist thing. We know that older people are more likely to exhibit chronic disease and need additional screening. So that would not be considered unethical in that context. Being able to visualize how these features impact your decisions is the goal of explainable AI."

Five Advantages of SHAP Scores

1. Model Agnostic

Can be applied to virtually any ML model (neural networks to linear models) and operates independently of internal workings.

2. Local Explanations

Provides explanations for individual predictions by calculating the contribution of each specific feature.

3. Unified Framework

Combines various existing methods (like LIME and DeepLIFT) under a single, understandable theoretical framework.

4. Feature Importance

Identifies the most influential features, enabling evaluation of domain context and potential responsibility concerns.

5. Model Validation

Provides insights into how different features interact and contribute to outcomes, supporting ongoing model validation efforts.

SHAP's Key Limitation: Interaction Effects

SHAP does not effectively capture interaction effects — scenarios where one variable behaves differently in the presence of another variable.

These interactions are often highly significant in complex models like neural networks. Because the SHAP graph evaluates features primarily in isolation, it misses these combined effects, meaning the explanation may be incomplete.

Complementary Approaches

SHAP's unified framework incorporates LIME — they are complementary, not competing.

LIME
  • Best for explaining individual predictions locally.
  • Excels at image region highlighting for visual models.
  • "Which exact areas of the eye drive the classification?"
SHAP
  • Best for understanding overall feature importance.
  • Excels at beeswarm visualization across entire datasets.
  • "How discriminatory is each health metric globally?"
