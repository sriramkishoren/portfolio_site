Before You Measure AI, Measure the Humans

Risk-Based Approach (Flawed)

  • • Evaluates AI against perfection[cite: 2]
  • • Assumes human performance is the gold standard
  • • AI often dismissed as "not good enough"
  • • Doesn't ask: How good are the humans?

Human Baseline Approach (Better)

  • • Evaluates AI against measured human performance[cite: 2]
  • • Measures how often humans actually agree
  • • Reveals AI may be closer to human-level than assumed
  • • Asks first: What is the human agreement rate?

5 Sources of Error Variance in Annotation[cite: 52]

These psychological and structural issues create the massive human disagreement that degrades AI performance.[cite: 80, 81]

1. Recognition[cite: 54]

Do annotators recognize the item to be labeled?[cite: 55]

Example: Labeling "Bob" but missing "he" as a person. "I didn't know we were doing pronouns."[cite: 57, 60]

2. Low Data by Class[cite: 63]

Rare events ("black swans") with low occurrence.[cite: 64]

Insufficient examples in the data negatively affect overall measurement and performance.[cite: 65, 66]

4. Within-Rater Reliability[cite: 75]

Same person, same data, different day.[cite: 76]

Internal consistency. How consistently can an individual repeat the exact same task?[cite: 77]

5. Between-Rater Reliability[cite: 78]

Two different people, same data.[cite: 78]

External consistency. Do individuals rate the exact same data consistently?[cite: 78]

Why Are Humans So Inconsistent?

These cognitive biases explain WHY the error sources exist.

Recognition & Attention

  • Inattentional Blindness: Missing items in plain sight. → Recognition Error
  • Confirmation Bias: Seeking info that confirms existing beliefs.

Classification & Judgment

  • Category Boundary Effects: Difficulty near boundaries. → Scale Complexity
  • Implicit Bias: Unconscious attitudes affecting labels.

Consistency Over Time

  • Decision Fatigue: Deterioration after long periods. → Within-Rater Reliab.
  • Drift: Unnoticed changes in criteria over time.

  • • Calculate marginal improvement
  • • Adopt where AI exceeds humans

Key Takeaways

1. Measure First. Evaluate AI against human performance, not perfection.

2. Massive Disagreement. Humans consistently disagree 17% to >30% on labeling tasks.

3. Beware the Gap. A 64% AI compared to a 68% human agreement rate is actually highly performant.

4. Scale Complexity Kills. Too much detail degrades AI because humans can't tell the difference.

5. Treat Errors Strategically. Collapsing labels to ancestor level boosts consistency.

6. Manage Vitality. Some annotators hurt consensus; removing them improves data quality.

7. Build in Spirals. Grow annotation incrementally to avoid technical debt.

8. Make Problems Visible. AI exposes human bias, giving us the chance to measure and fix it.

"What is the risk when we know that human processes are flawed and imperfect?"[cite: 131]

Measure. Compare. Improve. — That's the Human Baseline.
