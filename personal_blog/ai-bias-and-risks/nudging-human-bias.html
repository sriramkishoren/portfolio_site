Case Study 1: U.S. Patent and Trade Office

The Context & Goal

  • The U.S. Patent and Trade Office employs over 8,000 patent examiners.
  • A search engine was developed in partnership with Google to return the 10 most similar existing patents.
  • The primary goal is improving decision quality, not simply saving money or reducing staff.

The Debate: How to Present Findings?

Rank Order Only

Current Baseline.

Star Ratings

e.g., 5 stars = very similar.

Percentage Similarity

e.g., 90% or 60% similar.

Experimental Method: "Alternative World"

Because actual patent data was difficult to test fairly, a three-factor factorial design experiment used movie similarity as a proxy, crowdsourcing hundreds of respondents.

  • Result 1: Baseline confirmed; as similarity increases, novelty assessment decreases.
  • Result 2: Adding star or percentage ratings shifts judgment upward, implying more patents would be approved.
  • Result 3: Percentage similarity creates sharper discernment—making examiners more likely to assess novelty correctly.

Measured Impact of AI Nudging

14-17% Time Reduction
1,120 Labor Equivalents Saved
~$112M Annual Cost Savings
20-25% Improved Confidence

Impact on backlog: 100 to 130,000 patents a year.

Nuance: "You might want to have fewer patents. You might want to have more patents. You might want to have more discernment. So there's a lot of different criteria that go into this."

Case Study 2: Recruiting & Resume Anonymization

The Problem

Humans exhibit bias based on applicant names, gender, educational background, or university type.

These are considered unfair hiring practices.

The AI Solution

An AI-powered resume screening tool developed for a commercial client to redact and anonymize resumes.

  • Layer 1: Masking explicit identifiers (names, gender) is not enough.
  • Layer 2: Detecting implicit signals and writing features that indicate race or gender.
  • Layer 3: Generatively rewriting portions of the resume so reviewers cannot infer protected characteristics.

"They were able to... remove what they felt was human bias in the recruiting process." (Note: Clients can still preserve legitimate criteria like educational background if desired.)

Case Study 3: Judicial Sentencing

Judges' decisions can be influenced by personal prejudice and fatigue, resulting in inconsistent sentencing. An AI tool can analyze case files, criminal history, and extenuating circumstances to benchmark decisions.

Crucial Caveat

"You don't necessarily want AI rendering judicial decisions. I'm not saying that you replace human judges with AI judges."

5 Benefits of AI as an Anchoring Tool

Measurement

Measure outcomes and see if there's room for concern.

Appeals Support

May support whether an appeals court takes up a case.

Bias Nudging

Mitigates potential bias the judge may bring to the sentence.

Societal Values

Interject societal values and compare decisions to the popular baseline.

Anchoring with Discretion

Provides an anchor point while preserving latitude for extenuating circumstances.

Case Study 4: Loan Approval

Loan officers may favor applicants based on race, gender, or socioeconomic status, leading to unequal financial access. Even online applications have room for bias.

The AI Solution: Design algorithms to only use objective criteria (credit scores, repayment history, work history) to eliminate discriminatory practices.

Essential Safeguard

Implement quality control and human auditing to ensure the AI hasn't drifted or hallucinated into a discriminatory state.

The AI Nudging Spectrum

Synthesizing the case studies reveals a progression of AI involvement.

Level 1

Information Enhancement

Case: Patent Office

AI adds similarity ratings. Human makes all decisions. Nudge mechanism via additional context.

Level 2

Bias Signal Removal

Case: Recruiting

AI removes/rewrites bias triggers. Human decides on cleaned data. Nudge via controlling information.

Level 3

Decision Anchoring

Case: Judicial

AI provides a baseline. Human retains discretion. Nudge via setting a fairness anchor.

Level 4

Objective Decision Making

Case: Loan Approval

AI makes the decision on objective data. Human audits. Nudge via replacing subjective judgment.

The Ethical Tension

As we move up the spectrum: "When do we go too far?"
But simultaneously: "Is withholding AI itself unethical?"

Why Do Humans Need Nudging?

These cognitive biases explain WHY human decisions in the case studies above are unreliable.

Info Processing

  • Anchoring Bias: Over-relying on first info. → Patent & Judicial
  • Confirmation Bias: Seeking info that confirms existing beliefs.
  • Framing Effect: Influenced by presentation format. → Patent Experiment

Social Judgment

  • Implicit Bias: Unconscious stereotypes affecting decisions. → Recruiting & Loans
  • Affinity Bias: Favoring people similar to oneself.
  • Halo Effect: One positive trait (e.g., prestigious university) overly influencing judgment.

Decision Fatigue

  • Decision Fatigue: Quality drops after long periods of deciding. → Judicial
  • Status Quo Bias: Preference for the current state.
  • Sunk Cost Fallacy: Continuing a course due to past investment.

A Framework for Responsible Nudging

Practical actions to address the ethical tensions of deploying AI.

Transparency

  • • Disclose AI influence
  • • Make recommendations explainable
  • • Clarify AI's exact role
  • • Allow human overrides

Fairness

  • • Regularly audit for drift/bias
  • • Test across demographics
  • • Establish expert feedback loops
  • • Monitor for human over-reliance

Context

  • • Match autonomy to stakes
  • • Require more oversight for high stakes
  • • Preserve discretion for extenuating circumstances

Evaluation

  • • Measure quality before/after
  • • Track demographic outcomes
  • • Run empirical tests (don't assume)
  • • Iterate continually

Key Takeaways

Human bias is real and measurable in professional sectors.
AI nudges through enhancement, removal, anchoring, and objectivity.
Patent Office experiment proved similarity ratings improve confidence and save millions.
Resume anonymization generatively rewrites to prevent biased inference.
AI anchors high-stakes decisions (Judicial), preserving human discretion.
AI may be fairer than humans for objective decisions (Loans) if audited properly.
Withholding AI is not the solution. We must think about HOW to incorporate it, not IF.
