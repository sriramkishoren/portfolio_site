The Overreaction Warning

Society overreacts to security issues. Once you overreact, it's very difficult to back off.

The Airport Security Analogy: Following 9/11, society instituted massive security overreactions that are arguably excessive and cost taxpayers billions. Who takes the risk of backing off? If we are not proactive, we end up living with bad policy.

Global Landscape

Europe's GDPR: Individual Rights

Europe's General Data Protection Regulation guarantees seven core rights to individuals.

Right to Access

Individuals can access their personal data held by organizations.

Reality: Complex bureaucratic requests that strain organizational resources.

Right to Rectification

Right to correct inaccurate or incomplete data.

Reality: Creates massive engineering overhead for immutable systems.

Right to Erasure

The "Right to be Forgotten" — request data deletion.

Reality: Technically impossible in many backups and machine learning models.

Restrict Processing

Prevent use of data under certain circumstances.

Reality: Disconnects user data dynamically, breaking app features.

Data Portability

Receive data in a structured format to send elsewhere.

Reality: Competitors rarely align on "commonly used" structural formats.

Right to Object

Object to processing for direct marketing or specific reasons.

Reality: The cause of annoying cookie banners and check boxes everywhere.

Automated Decisions

Rights regarding automated policing, loan approvals, and resume reviews.

Reality: Severely limits AI development in European markets.

The Lecturer's View

"At first blush, they seem great. In practice: annoying little check blocks on every website."

The AI Arms Race Warning

"We are in a global AI arms race to develop more sophisticated models to crush competition. Being inhibited in your ability to develop better AI models has a risk as well. I would argue GDPR is an example of excessive regulation that is not necessarily achieving its true intent."

United States: CalOPPA & The National Standard

CA

The National Standard Effect

California's law effectively set the national baseline. Companies adopt it nationwide to avoid managing different regional software versions.

"While there aren't many broad federal laws yet, it's likely the U.S. will adopt some in the near future."

Privacy Policy

Must be prominently posted on the website.

PII Disclosure

Explicit details on data usage and sharing.

Review Process

Users can review and request changes.

Effective Date

Policy requires a clear effective date.

Do-Not-Track

Must define response to tracking opt-outs.

30-Day Cure

30 days to fix violations after notification.

China's PIPL: The Dual System

China's Personal Information Protection Law (PIPL)

A system of individual rights overridden by broad government powers.

Individual Rights

On Paper

"At first glance, this seems like great policy. It gives people reasonable rights without an onerous burden."

  • Know & Be InformedHow personal info is processed
  • Decide & ConsentAbility to withdraw consent
  • Access & CopyRequest access and obtain copies
  • Correct & DeleteFix or remove inaccurate data
  • PortabilityTransfer data to another service
BUT

Government Exemptions

In Practice
THERE IS A BUT...
  • National Security Exemption

    Broad exemption allowing facial recognition and social media surveillance to "keep population in line."

  • Cybersecurity Laws (2017)

    Mandates networks must cooperate with security agencies for surveillance.

  • Public Security Laws

    Broad, invasive authority enabling AI for crime prevention and public order monitoring.

  • State-Controlled Entities

    State-owned platforms bypass protection laws entirely, subject only to direct government control.

Core Privacy Principles

Foundational concepts underpinning all frameworks.

Data Minimization

Collect only what is necessary (GDPR default).

Purpose Limitation

Use only for stated intent (Consent basis).

Data Residency & Transfer

Keeping data in-border; strict export rules.

Privacy-Enhancing Tech

Tools to balance protection with AI development.

Differential Privacy

Adds noise so individuals can't be identified.

Federated Learning

Trains AI on distributed data without centralizing it.

Homomorphic Encryption

Computes on encrypted data directly.
