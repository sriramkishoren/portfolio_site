Image Generation - History and GANs

From the Utah Teapot to Photorealistic AI Art

Explore the 50-year evolution of synthetic images, the three primary model families, and the intricate architecture of Generative Adversarial Networks.

Source: Dr. Ian McCullough, Johns Hopkins University

The Core Framework

Synthetic images and video can be generated in the same manner as generative text. While text generation produces words, image generation produces pixels. Both rely on learning patterns from training data and producing new content. The key differences are the type of pre-processing, the data, and the model itself. Understanding the differences, the vocabulary, and how each works is essential.

The Three Pillars of Image Generation

The most common models—understand the difference, the vocabulary, and the basic way image generation works. Same goal, different approaches.

GAN

Generative Adversarial Network

Generator vs. Discriminator compete

  • Produces sharp images
  • Can be unstable to train
  • Introduced in 2014

VAE

Variational Autoencoder

Encoder → Latent Space → Decoder

  • Stable training process
  • Output can be blurrier
  • Introduced in 2014

Diffusion Model

State-of-the-Art Paradigm

Add noise → Learn to reverse it

  • Highest quality outputs
  • Computationally expensive
  • Mainstreamed ~2020

The Evolution of Generative Images

~50 Years of Progress

Era 1: Symbolic AI

1975 — The Utah Teapot

"The first example of a generative image" — symbolic Gen.AI using rules for light, shadows, and 3D rotation. Completely hand-coded knowledge.

Era 2: Early Neural Generation

2006 — Deep Belief Networks

"Can we generate numbers?" Created handwritten digits with varying quality depending on tuning and layers.

~2009 — Convolutional Deep Belief Nets

CNNs introduced. Marked "the beginnings of facial generation"—producing recognizable features like noses, eyes, chins, and ears.

Era 3: The GAN Revolution

2014 — VAE & GAN

Variational Autoencoders made faces more accurate. GANs introduced a different adversarial mechanism.

DCGAN

"Take images and combine them" via mathematical concatenation (e.g., adding sunglasses).

Pixel RNN

"Complete occluded parts of an image with what's most likely" — the birth of AI inpainting.

StyleGAN & BigGAN

StyleGAN created highly individual faces; BigGAN focused on "resolution boosting".

Era 4: Text-to-Image Revolution

2020 — ImageGPT & CLIP

Transformer-based creation. CLIP helped map text to image in various contextual styles.

DALL-E

"Zero-shot image generation." The model became large enough to generate novel images from prompts without specific task training data.

Stable Diffusion & ControlNet

"State of the art." Realistic images and video. ControlNet allowed character posing to "avoid model degeneracy" and produce consistent results.

Symbolic AI (1975)

  • Rules-based
  • "Using rules to understand light and objects"
  • Hand-coded knowledge; limited to what was explicitly programmed.
  • Example: Utah Teapot

Neural Networks (2006+)

  • Data-driven
  • "Learning patterns from examples"
  • Learned knowledge; generalizes patterns to create entirely new images.
  • Example: Deep Belief Nets → GANs

CLIP (Multi-Style)

Moves from text to image in different styles (e.g., bananas represented as sketches vs. photos in different contexts).

DALL-E (Zero-Shot)

"Don't have to give it training data." The model is large enough to generate novel images solely from a text prompt.

Stable Diffusion

Solving Model Degeneracy

THE PROBLEM:

"Issues with model degeneracy or failure to converge on an image." (Inconsistent outputs, loss of detail).

THE SOLUTION: CONTROLNET

The most advanced (at time of recording). A form of Stable Diffusion that adds structural guidance.

  • Pose your characters
  • Make them look much more realistic
  • Work better and more consistently

"Image generation has evolved dramatically over nearly 50 years — from the Utah Teapot's rules-based rendering in 1975 to today's AI models that create photorealistic images from text descriptions. The three major model families — GANs, Variational Autoencoders, and Diffusion Models — each take different approaches to the same goal. The GAN architecture, with its adversarial game between generator and discriminator, demonstrated that unsupervised learning could produce stunningly realistic images. From synthetic data generation for military training to super resolution for image enhancement, from removing photobombers to creating deep fakes, the applications of image generation are as varied as they are powerful. As this technology continues to advance — from ControlNet's posed characters to zero-shot text-to-image generation — the ability to understand how these models work, their capabilities, and their ethical implications becomes essential for anyone working with AI."
