Image Generation - VAE and Diffusion Models

From Latent Spaces to Denoising: Two Pillars of Modern Image Generation

Having explored GANs, we now turn to the other two major model families. Variational Autoencoders learn compressed representations in latent space and generate by sampling from them. Diffusion Models learn to reverse a gradual noise-adding process, producing state-of-the-art image quality. Understanding both is essential to mastering image generation.

Forward vs. Reverse Diffusion

"Adding noise and then removing noise — going back and forth"

THE FORWARD PROCESS (Destruction)
Clean → Noisy → Noisier → Pure Noise
(Fixed schedule — no learning needed)
THE REVERSE PROCESS (Reconstruction)
Pure Noise → Cleaner → Cleaner → Clean
(Learned by neural network — the hard part)

VAE vs. Diffusion Models

Variational Autoencoder

  • How it works: Encoder → Latent Space → Decoder
  • Generates by: Sampling from continuous latent space → decode
  • Speed: Fast (single decoder pass)
  • Quality: Can be blurrier (averaging effect of loss function)
  • Best For: Search, interpolation, representational learning

Diffusion Model

  • How it works: Add noise step by step → learn to reverse it
  • Generates by: Starting from pure noise and denoising step by step
  • Speed: Slower (requires many denoising steps)
  • Quality: "Coherent, realistic" — State of the art
  • Best For: Text-to-image, super resolution, object detection
MODERN BEST OF BOTH WORLDS: Latent Diffusion
Systems like Stable Diffusion use a VAE to compress images, then run the diffusion process in the compressed latent space.

Efficiency

"Diffusion models have a lower relative cost computationally than some of these other models."

  • GANs: Unstable, two networks competing.
  • VAEs: Tension between reconstruction & regularization.
  • Diffusion: Simple training objective (remove noise), stable convergence.
  • Latent Diffusion: Runs in compressed space, usable on consumer GPUs.

The Modern Image Generation Landscape

Image generation is an incredibly interesting and rapidly growing field. Variational Autoencoders give us a powerful framework for understanding images through latent space — enabling interpolation, search, anomaly detection, and generation by learning compressed representations of visual data.

Diffusion Models represent the current state of the art, producing the most coherent and realistic images by learning to reverse a gradual noise-adding process — one tiny denoising step at a time, sculpting images from pure random noise.

Together, these two model families — often combined in systems like Stable Diffusion — power modern image generation, super resolution, object detection, and controlled generation through tools like ControlNet. The ability to move continuously along the noise-to-quality spectrum gives us remarkable power in not just image generation, but image processing and AI vision.
