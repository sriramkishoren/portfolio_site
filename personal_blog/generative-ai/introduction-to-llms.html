Understanding Large Language Models

There are a number of large language models out there—ChatGPT, Llama 2, Falcon, Gemini, Copilot, Google BERT—each with different strengths and approaches. While some excel at creative content, others use different underlying architectures meant for document understanding. Let's explore how these models actually work.

Source Verification: The Moons Investigation

Generative search provides source references, which is vital because AI can be wrong. This enables users to interrogate and verify results.

1
"Which planet has the most moons?"

Traditional search yields conflicting sources: space.com says Jupiter, Kiwi.com says Saturn. Where do you draw knowledge from?

2
ChatGPT Says Jupiter

It returns Jupiter (95 moons), explicitly citing NASA. But Kiwi.com claimed Saturn has 145. "I question the information."

3
User Challenges AI

The user interrogates the model: "Why did you say 83 confirmed moons when the NASA website you referenced says 146?"

4
Self-Correction

ChatGPT admits error: "You're correct. Saturn has 146..." The sourcing allowed verification and correction.

The Benefit: Adaptation

  • Search returns dynamically improve and update over time.
  • Models are not permanently stuck returning outdated information.
  • Provides contextual understanding and explicit references.

The Risk: Data Poisoning

  • Bad actors can "poison" search returns.
  • Flooding models with misleading requests or biased content.
  • Directing the model to rely on a specific, flawed body of knowledge.

The Complete Training Pipeline

A raw pre-trained LLM is rarely helpful on its own. It requires a three-step process driven by human intervention to generate high-quality outputs.

Pre-Trained Large Language Model
1

Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)

Prompts are sampled from a dataset, and human labelers demonstrate what the desired output behavior should look like.

Action: Fine-tune the base model with supervised learning based on these high-quality human demonstrations.
2

Reward Model Training

The model generates multiple different responses (A, B, C, D) for a prompt. Human rankers evaluate and rank them from best to worst.

Action: Train a reward model to score outputs, teaching the AI what humans consider a "better" response.
3

PPO Reinforcement Learning

Using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), the system generates an output, the reward model calculates a score, and the policy updates.

Action: Optimize the model iteratively using reinforcement learning to maximize output quality.

The Crucial "Human-in-the-Loop"

"There's a lot that is being done by people in that process to get to a high quality output." Models don't just grow randomly; they are meticulously guided.

Demonstrators

Provide high-quality examples of desired behaviors to set the standard.

Rankers

Employees or users who judge responses from best to worst to train the reward model.

Users

Interact, verify, challenge, and organically improve the system over time.

Key Advantages

Contextual Understanding
Grasps user intent via vectors, ignoring pure keyword limitations.
Source Attribution
Links directly to knowledge sources (like NASA) allowing verification.
Interrogation
Users can challenge discrepancies and demand clarification naturally.

Risks & Limits

Inherent Inaccuracy
"Any search is not perfect... there's always room for error." Hallucination is real.
Data Poisoning
Malicious floods of requests can bias or poison the learning model.
Data & Privacy Concerns
Unclear disparities in terms of service regarding search returns and user data handling.
