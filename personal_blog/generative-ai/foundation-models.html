The Power of Foundation Models

Massive data, enormous models, and the architecture behind modern AI.

Foundation models are essential for generative AI and for understanding how transformers operate in artificial intelligence. The basic idea is that there are a ton of different data sources that you can pull in, bundled together into a single architecture.

A foundation model is understanding a lot of things about a particular domain or application area—patterns that are common to a lot of different tasks. From that single model, we can then apply that knowledge to a variety of different tasks based on simple prompts.

Key Infrastructure Insights:

  • Training data sets are far larger than anything previously used to train an AI model.
  • This massive scale is strictly enabled by modern cloud computing infrastructure.
  • The models are enormous—the number of parameters and size can be quite large, into the billions.
Let's explore what makes foundation models work.

The Transformer Revolution

Groundbreaking Milestone in AI

"Attention Is All You Need"

— Paper introduced by Google in 2017

Transfer Learning

Take a model trained in one domain, and then just do a little bit of extra work to help fine-tune it to a different application. You are transforming the model by adjusting some of the weights to do a different task. (Note: Not the cartoon robots!)

Attention Mechanism

The core concept is requiring the model to attend differently to different words or different areas of the data so that it can perform a new task effectively based on context.

Computational Advantages of Attention Models

Parallelization

Split jobs into different components that can run simultaneously instead of waiting for sequential completion, significantly accelerating computational speed.

Memory Efficiency

Focus only on the words you need instead of having to attend to all of the data. Focus strictly on what's important.

Adaptive Focus

Dynamically adjust weighting to different parts of the input. Higher relevance words receive more attention.

Scalable I/O Length

Handle varying lengths of input and output. These advantages allow tasks impossible without narrowing scope and focusing computations.

Self-Attention In Action

CONTEXTUAL UNDERSTANDING

the cat sat on the mat
HIGH WEIGHT (Who?)
HIGH WEIGHT (Where?)

Context changes meaning: The word sat is referring to the cat (it wasn't the mat sitting on the cat). Where did it sit? On the mat.

Neighboring influence: This conceptual understanding uses other words in the sentence to help provide contextual meaning for the word you are looking at.

Selective Weighting: Process all words simultaneously as separate jobs. Weight "cat", "sat", and "mat" higher (more informative) and weight "the" or "on" lower.

Context Determines Meaning

Same Word. Different Context. Different Meaning.

Definition 1: Spherical Object

The dog chased after the ball as it rolled down the hill.

Evidence gathered by Transformer:
The words dog and rolled provide evidence that this is a spherical object you throw.

Definition 2: Formal Dance Party

Cinderella went to the ball in a fancy glass coach.

Evidence gathered by Transformer:
The words Cinderella and fancy provide evidence suggesting this is a formal event.

Token Prediction & Text Generation

Because self-attention correctly classifies context, transformer networks can successfully predict tokens (additional words) within the context of other words. Each next word is a probabilistic expression of what is a common next word in that sentence.

"The dog chased after the ball..."
P(next): as (35%) | and (20%) | down (15%)
"The dog chased after the ball as..."
P(next): it (45%) | the (15%)
"The dog chased after the ball as it..."

Different contexts give a different understanding of a word, likely resulting in a completely different sequence of generated words. Self-attention + transformers = classify AND generate.

Vector Embeddings & Semantic Search

The mathematical foundation mapping meaning. An embedding model converts a series of objects into vectors of different weights. Words become a vector space with different probabilities, allowing models to generate new words that sound like how people actually write (because it's trained on a lot of writings).

Vector Similarity Space

Key Takeaways

Enormous Scale: Massive diverse training data, billions of parameters, and staggering energy demands requiring cloud infrastructure.
Transformers (2017): The architecture enabling transfer learning and the revolutionary attention mechanism.
